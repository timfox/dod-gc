# Fact check: ep01-omaha

Checked 2026-11-19 against the episode's sources. Every spoken line, card and lower third is listed with what
supports it. Lines changed by this check say what they said before.

Sources:
- **CMH**: U.S. Army Center of Military History, *Omaha Beachhead* (1945), CMH Pub 100-11
  ([online](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-A-Omaha/index.html)), chapters 2 (beach), 5 (end of D-Day).
- **ABMC**: American Battle Monuments Commission, Normandy American Cemetery.
- **NWWII**: The National WWII Museum, [D-Day research starter](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/students-teachers/student-resources/research-starters/research-starters-d-day).
- **EB**: Encyclopaedia Britannica, [Omaha Beach](https://www.britannica.com/place/Omaha-Beach).
- **DD tanks**: 741st Tank Battalion launch at Omaha, summarised at [dday-overlord.com](https://www.dday-overlord.com/?p=220359); also EB.

## Narration

| Shot | Line | Verdict | Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| s01 | Before dawn on the sixth of June 1944, the largest seaborne invasion in history was under way off the coast of Normandy. | OK | NWWII ("largest amphibious invasion in history"); the fleet crossed overnight. |
| s02 | Allied troops would land on five beaches, code-named Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno and Sword. | OK | NWWII, EB. |
| s03 | Omaha, about **six and a half kilometres** of sand beneath steep bluffs, was assigned to the United States 1st and 29th Infantry Divisions. | **Changed** (was "about eight kilometres") | CMH ch. 2: the part suitable for landing "was about 7,000 yards long" (6.4 km); "bluffs 100 to 170 feet in height". Popular figures run 8 to 10 km because they measure the whole coast between Port-en-Bessin and the Vire. EB: the 1st Division led, with the 29th's 116th Regiment attached. |
| s04 | The first assault waves came ashore at around half past six in the morning, into heavy fire. | OK | EB, CMH: H-Hour 0630. |
| s05 | From the bluffs, German positions overlooked the whole length of the beach. | OK | CMH ch. 2: the bluffs "dominate the whole beach area". |
| s06 | Rough seas and currents pushed many landing craft off course. **Of twenty-nine swimming tanks launched offshore, twenty-seven sank.** | **Changed** (was "...and much of the supporting armour never reached the shore") | The old line overstated it: the 743rd Tank Battalion's tanks were landed straight onto the beach and dozens were in action by mid-morning. What did happen: the 741st launched 29 DD tanks at sea and 27 sank (EB, DD tanks source). Smoke was dropped from the line for length, not because it was wrong. |
| s07 | **From around eight in the morning**, small groups, often led by junior officers and sergeants, began to work their way up the bluffs between the strongpoints. | **Changed** (was "By mid-morning") | CMH ch. 5 puts the first penetrations of the bluffs "between 0800 and 0900". |
| s08 | **Through the day, the strongpoints above the beach began to fall.** | **Changed** (was "One by one, the defences above the beach were taken.") | CMH ch. 5: at nightfall "cut-off enemy groups were still resisting"; the old line implied all were taken. |
| s09 | By nightfall they held a strip **at most two and a half kilometres deep**. | **Changed** (was "only about two kilometres deep") | CMH ch. 5: near Colleville "hardly more than a mile-and-a-half deep" (2.4 km), shallower west of St-Laurent. |
| s09 | **Around three thousand men were killed, wounded or missing**, more than on any other D-Day beach. | **Changed** (was "an estimated two thousand four hundred casualties") | CMH ch. 5, from corrected returns: V Corps losses "in the neighborhood of 3,000 killed, wounded, and missing". EB and many popular accounts give 2,400 for Omaha; estimates vary, which "around" covers. Utah, Gold, Juno and Sword each had fewer. |
| s10 | Today the Normandy American Cemetery at Colleville-sur-Mer stands on the bluff above Omaha Beach. | OK | ABMC. |
| s10 | More than nine thousand Americans are buried there. | OK | ABMC: 9,386 to 9,389 burials depending on the count's date. |

## ADR

The callouts are generic and invented for atmosphere; none claims to be a recorded or attributed quote, and
no real person is named or voiced. Checked for period plausibility:

- "Medic!" was the US Army call for an aid man. "Shingle" for the stone bank is the term CMH uses.
- "Step where I step. Watch for mines.": the bluffs and draws were mined (CMH ch. 2 and 4).
- Radio "...Over." follows period voice procedure. The German "Da unten! Am Strand, links!" ("Down there! On
  the beach, left!") is plain spoken German, subtitled.
